OpenDiag

Free, open-source vehicle diagnostics for Windows and Android

Test build: please help us test it

OpenDiag reads fault codes, readiness, live data and vehicle information through an ordinary OBD-II adapter, and works fully offline. Everything stays on your device. This is an early test build: almost nothing has been confirmed on real hardware yet, and that is what we need your help with.

What you need

  • An ELM327-compatible adapter (USB, Bluetooth or Wi-Fi). Genuine ELM327 v1.4b+ or OBDLink/STN adapters work best; cheap "v1.5" clones vary.
  • A vehicle with an OBD-II socket (most petrol cars from 2001 in Europe, 1996 in the US; diesels from about 2004).
  • For older cars and motorcycles: a K-line (KKL) USB cable. Trucks, buses and machines: a 9-pin to 16-pin cable (see Find my connector in the app).
  • No vehicle? Choose Connect → Simulator to try everything with a simulated car, motorcycle or truck.

How to test

  1. Install and open OpenDiag, and read the short risk notice.
  2. Plug the adapter in, switch the ignition on (engine off is fine) and go to Connect. USB: if no port appears, install the driver for the adapter's chip (FTDI, CH340 or CP210x). Bluetooth on Windows: pair it in Settings first.
  3. Open Hardware test and follow it. It checks the connection, scan, live data and reports, and asks you a few yes/no questions.
  4. At the end press Copy report and paste it into a compatibility report with the vehicle's make, model and year and your adapter's name.
  5. Something broke or looked wrong? Report a problem, with a screenshot if you can.
Clearing fault codes is optional and only offered with the engine off, after the codes are saved. OpenDiag never programs or codes control units. Use it only on vehicles you own or may work on.
